A Brief History of Instruments

Long before written music existed, early humans were already shaping sound — striking hollow logs, stretching animal skin over frames, and carving simple flutes from bone. These first instruments weren't built for concerts; they were built for ritual, storytelling, and communication.

As civilizations grew, so did the instruments themselves. Stringed instruments like the lyre and lute appeared in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, while early forms of brass and woodwind instruments developed in parallel across different cultures. Each new material and technique — metalworking, tuning systems, keyed mechanisms — pushed instruments further from simple tools toward the precise, expressive machines musicians use today.

By the time of the Renaissance and Baroque eras, instrument-making had become its own craft, with families of strings, brass, woodwind, and keyboard instruments each evolving their own traditions. That same family structure is still how we organize and understand instruments now.
