How Technology Changed Music

From the synthesiser to the streaming platform — how innovation reshaped the way music is made, sold, and heard.

An industry rebuilt by its tools

Every era of music has been shaped by the technology available to it. Synthesisers gave artists entirely new sounds to work with, digital production software put a studio on every laptop, and streaming platforms rewrote how audiences discover and pay for music. This page traces that shift — the instruments, the software, and the platforms that changed the industry.

1980s

Affordable synthesisers like the Yamaha DX7 bring electronic sound into the mainstream.

2008

Spotify launches, kicking off the shift from ownership to on-demand streaming.

Today

AI-assisted production tools are changing how songs are written, mixed, and mastered.

Key Shifts in the Industry

A Timeline of Change

1970s

Analog synthesisers become widely available, giving rise to entirely new genres.

1990s

The MP3 format compresses audio files small enough to share online, laying the groundwork for digital music.

2001

iTunes launches, offering legal digital downloads and reshaping music retail.

2008

Spotify brings streaming to the mainstream, shifting the industry from ownership to access.

2020s

AI-powered tools enter production workflows, from mastering to full track generation.
