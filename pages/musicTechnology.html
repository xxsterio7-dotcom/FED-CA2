How Technology Changed Music

From the synthesiser to the streaming platform — how innovation reshaped the way music is made, sold, and heard.

An industry rebuilt by its tools

Every era of music has been shaped by the technology available to it. Synthesisers gave artists entirely new sounds to work with, digital production software put a studio on every laptop, and streaming platforms rewrote how audiences discover and pay for music.

1980s

Affordable synthesisers like the Yamaha DX7 bring electronic sound into the mainstream.

2008

Spotify launches, kicking off the shift from ownership to on-demand streaming.

Today

AI-assisted production tools are changing how songs are written, mixed, and mastered.

Key Technologies in Music Creation

How listening to music evolved

1940s-1970s

Vinyl Records

Vinyl manufacturers would press a long winding groove around a small plastic disc, known as a vinyl. Listeners would then purchase these vinyl records and play them using a turntable, or even collect them as rare collectibles

1970s-1980s

Cassette Tapes

Cassette tapes became a much more portable and popular option to the vinyl tape. These compact strips of black tape could be played on the go with casette players such as the Walkman

1980s-2000s

Compact Discs

Compact Discs, or better known as CDs, brought digital audio formats into the mainstream, by storing binary data as grooves on it's surface. CDs, compared to vinyl or cassette, offered clearer sound and were much more durable.

2000s-2010s

MP3 players

Portable MP3 players were a revolutionary step into the digital age of music. Consumers were able to store thousands of songs, curate playlists and search for individual tracks, all in a tiny little screen that they could slide into their pockets.

2010s-Now

Streaming Platforms

Streaming now dominates how we listen to music today. Platforms, like Spotify and Apple Music, provide access to millions of songs and albums at the click of a button. Instead of a owned music collection, users instead gain access to an enormous library of music, shifting our perspective on how we interact with music
